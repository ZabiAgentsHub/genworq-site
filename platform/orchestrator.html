AI Orchestration

The system that decides who does what, and when a human should.

Every task passes through the orchestrator first: read, checked against your rules, then handled or handed to a person.

The GENWORQ orchestrator is the coordination layer that routes incoming work to the right agent, checks it against your approval rules, and logs every decision. The system every GENWORQ solution runs on top of.

How it decides

What is the orchestrator actually checking?

Trigger

Work arrives the way it already does: a form fill, an inbound call, an invoice landing in the inbox, a record changing in your CRM. The orchestrator watches those entry points across CRM, ERP, Notify, Analytics, Docs and Inbox, and treats each one as a trigger rather than something a person has to notice first.

Understand

Before anything moves, it works out what the task actually is: who it came from, what it relates to, and what the equivalent job looked like last time. It reads that context out of the systems you already run rather than asking someone to supply it.

Decide

Then it picks the agent whose job this is and checks the work against your rules. This is where the approval points you set come into play: anything strategic, sensitive or irreversible stops here rather than carrying on.

Act

If it clears your rules, the agent does the work: drafting the reply, matching the invoice, updating the record. Every action is logged and reversible, and anything the system is not confident about gets held for a person instead of guessed at.

Improve

What ran, when, and why is written down. That record is what lets you see the hours saved and the mistakes avoided in plain numbers, rather than taking it on trust. Rule changes go through the same approval points as everything else. Nothing changes silently.

Common questions

What teams ask about the orchestrator.

What exactly does the orchestrator decide on its own, versus escalate?

It handles the work that clears your rules and escalates everything else. Strategic, sensitive or irreversible actions stop at the approval points you set, and anything the system is not confident about gets held for a person instead of guessed at.

Can we set our own approval thresholds?

Yes. You define the policy, access and operational constraints before agents take action, and you decide which steps always need sign-off. You configure them directly, and only the people you designate can change them.

What happens if it’s wrong?

Every action is logged and reversible. You can see what the agent used, why it decided, and where the work moved next, so a wrong call can be traced and undone rather than found out about later.

Does every action get logged, or just the ones that get escalated?

Every action. The decision trail covers what ran, not only what stopped for a human. That record is what teams use to check what happened instead of taking it on trust.
