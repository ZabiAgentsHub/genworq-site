The cost of running it by hand

Is manual work capping your company’s growth?

For most companies the ceiling is not demand. It is the number of hours people can spend re-keying data, chasing approvals and rebuilding the same report. Four patterns show up again and again, and every one of them gets worse as the business grows.

Repetitive work eats the week

The work that keeps a company running is also the work that never finishes: the same figures typed into a second system, the approval chased twice, the report rebuilt from scratch every month.

  • The same record typed into two or three tools
  • Approvals chased by email, then chased again
  • Month-end assembled by hand, every month

Planning model: a fifth of the working year on work nobody chose to do

Media breaks and system silos

Every hand-off between an inbox, a spreadsheet and an ERP is a point where a record changes shape. The data survives the trip; the meaning often does not.

  • Order details re-keyed from an email into the ERP
  • A spreadsheet that only one department trusts
  • Updates that never travel back to the system of record

Typical cost: the same record in three states, and no way to tell which one is right

No cost transparency

Ask what it costs to process one purchase order or one onboarding, and the honest answer is usually a shrug. Without a figure per run, nothing can be prioritised and nothing can be proved.

  • No measured cost or cycle time per process
  • Overtime treated as normal, not as a signal
  • Improvements argued from opinion, not numbers

Typical cost: decisions made on a feeling, because the number does not exist

Staff shortage and key-person risk

One person knows how the process really works, including the exceptions that were never written down. When they are on leave, the queue simply grows.

  • Process knowledge that lives in one head
  • Backlogs that build during holidays and sickness
  • New starters trained on habits, not on a documented flow

Typical cost: the process stops when one person is away

Our approach

Automate the process. Then scale it.

Genworq automates a business process in three moves: connect the systems that already hold the work, run the process end to end with AI agents, and put every step on a board a person can audit. The order matters. Visibility is what makes the second and third process an easy decision.

Workflows

Automate the whole workflow, not one task

A macro on somebody’s laptop automates a keystroke. We automate the process: the trigger that starts it, the systems it reads, the rules it follows and the record it writes back when it is done.

  • One process end to end, not a shortcut on one machine
  • Runs on its trigger, at 9pm on a Friday as readily as Tuesday morning
  • Exceptions routed to the person who can actually decide
Low risk

Optimise processes without an IT project

The pilot runs beside the manual process, not instead of it, so you can compare the two on real volumes for a fortnight before anything is switched over. Nothing is ripped out, and nothing is irreversible.

  • Starts as one process, next to the way you run it today
  • Your systems stay exactly where they are
  • Switched off in one click if it does not earn its place
Transparency

See every AI-supported step, in plain numbers

One board shows what ran, what it touched, what it saved and what is waiting on a human. The Orchestrator keeps the decision trail behind it, so an auditor can follow any single run from trigger to result.

  • Volume, cycle time and error rate visible without asking for a report
  • Every action carries a name, a timestamp and a reason
  • You choose which steps always wait for a human signature

    Common questions

    Questions teams ask before their first automation project

    What is business process automation?

    Business process automation is the use of software to run repeatable, rule-based work end to end, order handling, invoicing, onboarding and procurement, without a person doing each step by hand. A trigger starts the process, the software reads the data it needs from your existing systems, applies the rules you agreed, writes the result back and logs what it did. The point is not to speed up typing; it is to remove the typing. At Genworq the work is carried out by AI agents, which means the process can also read an unstructured email or document and still finish, where a rigid script would stop.

    How is business process automation different from RPA?

    RPA copies a person’s clicks and keystrokes on a screen, while business process automation with AI agents works at the level of the process itself. An RPA bot breaks when a button moves or a supplier changes their invoice layout, because it only knows the surface. An agent reads the content, decides the next step against your rules and writes the result back through the system’s own interface, so a new layout is just another document. We still use straightforward scripted steps where the interface is stable and the rules are fixed, because a simple mechanism that cannot drift is often the right answer.

    Which processes should we automate first?

    Start with the process that has high volume, low judgement and clear rules, because that combination is where automation pays back fastest and fails least. In most companies the first candidates look like this:

    • ·Order handling: reading orders from email and creating them in the ERP
    • ·Invoicing and matching: raising invoices, matching payments, chasing overdue accounts
    • ·Onboarding: setting up a new customer, employee or supplier across several systems
    • ·Procurement: purchase requests, approvals and supplier record updates
    • ·Reporting: the weekly or monthly numbers somebody currently rebuilds by hand

    The job that eats the most hours and involves the least thinking is almost always the right place to begin.

    How long does it take to get the first process live?

    Most first processes go live in two to four weeks, measured from the first mapping session to the process running in production. Week one is analysis, week two is design and the start of the build, weeks three and four are integration, testing and tuning the exception paths. We deliberately start with one process rather than your whole operation, so you can see it working on real volumes before committing further. The second process is usually faster, because the connections to your CRM, inbox and accounts package are already built and tested.

    What happens when the automation gets something wrong?

    Every action is logged and reversible, and anything the system is not confident about is held for a person instead of guessed at. You decide up front which steps always need a signature, for example any refund over a threshold or any change to a supplier’s bank details. When a run is held, it stops at that step with the reason attached, so the person picking it up has the context rather than a mystery. Errors are visible on the same board as successful runs, which is what makes the error rate a number you can manage rather than a feeling.

    Do we have to replace the software we already use?

    No. Genworq builds on top of your existing CRM, inbox, accounts package and spreadsheets, for example HubSpot, Microsoft 365, Slack, Supabase and n8n. Nothing gets thrown out and nobody has to learn a new system, because the work still lands in the tools your team already knows. That is deliberate: replacing a working system is a project, and projects are exactly what stops automation from ever starting. If a tool genuinely cannot be integrated, we will say so before you commit rather than after.

    Is business process automation GDPR-compliant?

    Genworq is GDPR-compliant by design: processing runs on EU infrastructure hosted in Frankfurt, access is role-based, and consent and retention rules are configured explicitly rather than assumed. Your data stays in your systems and stays yours. Every action an agent takes is recorded with a name, a timestamp and a reason, which is what makes a data subject request or an audit answerable rather than an archaeology project. Automated decision-making is documented as part of the process design, so your legal and works council review can happen before the pilot goes live.

    What does business process automation cost?

    Price is driven by two things: how many processes are in scope, and how many systems each one touches. A single process running across two or three systems sits at the low end; several processes spanning your accounts package, CRM and inbox sits higher. We scope it first, so you get a fixed price and an estimate of hours saved before any work starts. If the numbers do not clear the cost of the build, we will tell you that in the scoping call rather than after the invoice.
