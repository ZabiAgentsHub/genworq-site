    Multi-agent orchestration

    How does multi-agent orchestration work for e-commerce?

    One agent that does everything does nothing well. Genworq runs a small crew: each agent owns one part of the process and hands over cleanly, while the Orchestrator decides who acts, logs every step and holds the risky ones for a person.

    Concierge agent

    Recognises the request, identifies the customer and routes the case to the right specialist, or to a person when policy says so.

    Order agent

    Reads shop, carrier and payment data. Answers status, changes addresses and cancels within the window, then writes back to the order.

    Returns agent

    Checks the return policy, issues the label, triggers the refund once the parcel scans in, and files the complaint with a person when it is not routine.

    Process knowledge built in, not prompted in

    Return windows, refund methods, carrier cut-offs, payment-provider rules and the statutory right of withdrawal are encoded as rules the agents must follow. The language model drafts the reply; the rules decide what is allowed.

    Common questions

    Questions shop teams ask before automating service

    What is e-commerce customer service automation?

    E-commerce customer service automation is the use of AI agents to resolve the repeat requests an online shop receives, order status, returns, product advice and cancellations, by reading the order directly from the shop system and acting on it. The agent identifies the customer, looks up the order, applies your rules (return window, refund method, carrier cut-off) and writes the result back to the helpdesk and the shop. Anything outside the rules goes to a person with the full context attached. The difference from a FAQ chatbot is the write-back: the work gets done, not just described.

    Which shop requests can be automated completely?

    Most shops can fully automate the requests that are high-volume and rule-based. In practice that means:

    • ·Order status and delivery window (“where is my order?”)
    • ·Returns: window check, label, refund on scan-in
    • ·Cancellations and address changes inside the allowed window
    • ·Product questions answered from the catalogue: size, fit, stock, compatibility
    • ·Order placement and changes by phone

    Complaints about damaged goods, disputes and anything involving goodwill are pre-sorted by the agent and decided by a person.

    How does the agent know it is talking to the right customer?

    The agent authenticates before it reveals or changes anything. Depending on the channel it matches order number plus email or postcode, sends a one-time code to the address on the order, or uses the logged-in session on the website. The rules are yours: you decide which data needs which level of proof, and every lookup is logged with the method used.

    Does it replace our helpdesk or shop system?

    No. Genworq works on top of the systems you already run, for example Shopify, Shopware or WooCommerce for the shop, Zendesk, Freshdesk or HubSpot for tickets, and DHL, DPD or Sendcloud for shipping. The agents read from and write back to those systems through their own interfaces, so your team keeps working where it works today and nothing is migrated.

    How long until the first use case is live?

    A first use case such as order status or returns is typically live in two to four weeks. The first week is mapping the request types and the rules behind them, the second is connecting the shop, helpdesk and carrier, and the remaining time is a pilot on real tickets beside your team, with the agent handling more as the error rate is confirmed. Additional use cases reuse the same connections and go faster.

    How is customer data handled? Is it GDPR-compliant?

    Customer and order data stay in your systems. Genworq processes on EU infrastructure hosted in Frankfurt, with role-based access, explicit retention rules and a full log of every read and write an agent performs. Automated decisions, such as approving a return, are documented in the process design so your legal review happens before the pilot, not after. Consumer-law duties like the statutory right of withdrawal are encoded as rules, not left to the model.

    What does it cost?

    Price depends on the number of use cases in scope and the systems each one touches. One use case across a shop system and a helpdesk sits at the low end; several use cases spanning shop, helpdesk, carriers and payments sits higher. We scope it first against your real ticket volume, so you get a fixed price and an estimate of tickets resolved per month before anything is built.
